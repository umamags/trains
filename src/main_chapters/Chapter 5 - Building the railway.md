
Indian Railways is so large that **no single organization builds everything**. Instead, it's an ecosystem of specialized factories, public-sector manufacturers, research organizations, and private suppliers.

Here's how one Rajdhani train comes into existence.

The coaches you ride in are manufactured primarily by three production units:

* Integral Coach Factory (Chennai)

  * Oldest and largest coach factory
  * Produces LHB coaches, Vande Bharat trainsets, EMUs, and more
* Rail Coach Factory (Kapurthala)

  * Manufactures modern LHB passenger coaches
* Modern Coach Factory (Rae Bareli)

  * Focuses on modern stainless-steel coaches and trainsets

Private companies supply components such as:

* air-conditioning units
* doors
* seats
* windows
* braking systems
* electrical equipment

* steel rails
* sleepers

Rail welding plants then join shorter rails into long welded rails to provide smoother rides.

# 4. Sleepers

Sleepers are the concrete or steel beams beneath the rails.

They:

* support the rails
* maintain the correct gauge
* distribute the train's weight
* keep the track stable

Most new Indian routes use prestressed concrete sleepers manufactured by railway-approved plants across the country.

Switches (also called **points** or **turnouts**) allow trains to move from one track to another.

They are precision-engineered because they must:

* withstand thousands of train movements
* operate reliably in all weather
* integrate with signaling and interlocking systems

Specialized manufacturers produce the rails, movable point blades, crossings, and point machines used to operate them.

# Research & Design

This is where new railway technology is developed.

### Research Designs and Standards Organisation (RDSO)

RDSO is the engineering and research arm of Indian Railways.

It develops and approves:

* coach designs
* locomotive standards
* signaling systems
* bridges
* track standards
* braking systems
* testing procedures

Before a new coach or locomotive enters service, it is typically evaluated against RDSO standards.

---

# Production Organizations

Indian Railways has dedicated production units that manufacture rolling stock, while many components come from public- and private-sector suppliers.

Examples include:

* Integral Coach Factory
* Rail Coach Factory
* Modern Coach Factory
* Banaras Locomotive Works

Together, they produce thousands of coaches and hundreds of locomotives every year.

---

# Maintenance Organizations

Once a train enters service, it spends far more time being maintained than being built.

Maintenance is handled by different parts of Indian Railways:

* **Coaching depots** maintain passenger coaches.
* **Loco sheds** maintain locomotives.
* **Track maintenance teams** inspect rails, sleepers, ballast, and switches.
* **Signal & Telecommunication (S&T) departments** maintain signals, interlocking systems, track circuits, axle counters, and communication equipment.
* **Electrical departments** maintain overhead electrification and traction power systems.

# The Complete Supply Chain

```text
RDSO
│
├── Designs standards and specifications
│
├── Coach Factories
│     └── Passenger coaches
│
├── Locomotive Factories
│     └── Electric and diesel locomotives
│
├── Steel Plants
│     └── Rails
│
├── Sleeper Plants
│     └── Concrete sleepers
│
├── Switch Manufacturers
│     └── Points and crossings
│
└── Indian Railways Zones
      ├── Operate trains
      ├── Maintain tracks
      ├── Maintain locomotives
      ├── Maintain coaches
      └── Maintain signaling
```

A useful way to think about it is by analogy with aviation:

| Railway                               | Airline Equivalent                             |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| RDSO                                  | Aircraft certification and standards authority |
| Coach factories                       | Aircraft manufacturers                         |
| Locomotive factories                  | Engine manufacturers                           |
| Track infrastructure                  | Airports and runways                           |
| Signal & Telecommunication department | Air traffic control systems                    |
| Coaching depots & loco sheds          | Aircraft maintenance bases                     |
| Railway zones                         | Airlines operating the service                 |

This division of responsibilities allows Indian Railways to operate one of the world's largest rail networks while continuously building, maintaining, and upgrading its rolling stock and infrastructure.
